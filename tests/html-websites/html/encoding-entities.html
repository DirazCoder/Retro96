[EN-06] Raw High Bytes (Latin-1 bytes written directly in the file)

The following words are written as raw single bytes in the file, not as entities: café, über, mañana, §, ±, °, ç, ß, à, ö, ÿ.

Mixed line, all four should look identical (e with acute): raw é, entity é, decimal é, hex é: é é é é.

Raw punctuation and symbols: « bonjour », © 1996, ® registered, ½ cup, 2 × 3 = 6, 20 ÷ 4 = 5, ±5%, 25°C, ¡Hola!, ¿Qué?, 3² and 5³.

[EN-07] Non-Breaking Space Across A Wrap

The words joined by nbsp must move to the next line together with their partners rather than splitting at those spaces; the ordinary spaces in this sentence are allowed to break wherever needed. The paragraph sits in a 220 pixel wide cell so that wrapping is forced early and often and the glued pairs are easy to spot.

[EN-08] Consecutive Non-Breaking Spaces

Five non-breaking spaces in a row: start[     ]end, and ten of them: start[          ]end. Every one of them must render as visible width; they must not collapse to a single space.

Contrast with ordinary spaces, which do collapse: start[ ]end shows one space in a browser.

[EN-09] Entities In Attributes

The alt text of the little red square contains quot, amp, lt, gt and a decimal entity; any tooltip or fallback rendering must decode them.

[EN-12] Unknown And Malformed Entities

Unknown name: [&foobar;] must appear literally, as ampersand f-o-o-b-a-r semicolon.

Missing semicolon: [ ] and [©] (no trailing semicolon): the engines of the era still decoded these; strict ones print them literally.

Wrong case, since entity names are case-sensitive: [©] and [&Nbsp;] are undefined names and must print literally.

Broken numeric forms: [&#;] [&#x;] [&#zz;] [ ] must print literally rather than crashing the tokenizer.

Double ampersand: [&&] and [&#65;]: the first renders as two ampersands in lenient engines; the second renders as an ampersand followed by literal #65; characters.
